The bond trading desk relies on a legacy tool at `/usr/local/bin/bondsched` to compute
fixed-rate bond coupon schedules. The original source code is lost — only the stripped
binary remains. Your job is to build a Python replacement.

## Goal

Create `/app/bondsched.py`, runnable as `python3 /app/bondsched.py`, that reads one trade
specification line from stdin and writes the coupon schedule to stdout. Your output must
match the reference binary byte-for-byte (same stdout content and same exit status) for
any input of the kinds shown in `/app/sample.txt`.

## Interface

Input: one line on stdin with space-separated key=value pairs:
```
settle=YYYY-MM-DD maturity=YYYY-MM-DD freq=N daycount=XXX bdc=YYY holidays=YYYY-MM-DD,...
```

- `freq`: 1 (annual), 2 (semi-annual), or 4 (quarterly)
- `daycount`: 30U360, 30E360, ACT365, or ACTACT
- `bdc`: NONE, FOLLOWING, PRECEDING, MODFOLLOW, or MODPRECED
- `holidays`: comma-separated YYYY-MM-DD dates (may be empty)

Output: one line per coupon period:
```
N START END PAYMENT FRACTION
```

- `N`: 1-based period index (no zero-padding)
- `START`: unadjusted start date (YYYY-MM-DD)
- `END`: unadjusted end date (YYYY-MM-DD)
- `PAYMENT`: business-day-adjusted payment date (YYYY-MM-DD)
- `FRACTION`: accrual fraction with exactly 6 decimal places

Exit codes:
- 0: success
- 1: empty stdin
- 2: malformed input, with `ERROR: reason` on stderr

## Schedule construction rules

1. Coupon dates are generated backwards from the maturity date at `12/freq`-month intervals.
2. Accrual fractions are computed from the **unadjusted** start and end dates; the payment date is the **BDC-adjusted** end date.
3. Weekends (Saturday, Sunday) are always non-business days.
4. Listed holidays are also non-business days.
5. Output must match byte-for-byte — identical text on stdout and identical exit code.

## Important notes

- The artifact **must not** call the reference binary or any external program.
- The reference binary will **not** be available when the artifact is checked.
- The legacy tool was written in-house and may deviate from ISDA textbook conventions.
- Probe the binary systematically on the sample inputs and your own test cases to discover its exact behaviour.
- See `/app/sample.txt` for 18 sample trade specifications covering all parameter values.
